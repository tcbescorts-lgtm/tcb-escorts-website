Pilot Car Requirements, Certification and Insurance by State

Pilot car requirements are set state by state, so there is no single answer. This guide explains what varies and how to check the current rules for each state on your route, rather than publishing a chart that could go out of date.

By TCB Escorts LLC · October 19, 2026 · 6 min read

What varies between states

FHWA's pre-trip planning guidance lists the main differences: whether operators must be certified, what equipment they carry or display, and what insurance is required. Some states also require flagging credentials, TWIC cards or defensive driving courses. And the number of escorts a load needs depends on that state's permit.

Certification

Many states require pilot/escort certification, but not all do. FHWA's operator training material notes that certification lasts roughly three to five years depending on the state, and that not all states accept certification from other states. Even where an out-of-state certificate is accepted, the host state's rules still apply: University of Florida's training FAQ, for instance, says an operator may escort in Florida with another state's qualification but must still follow Florida's requirements.

Reciprocity agreements change, so rely on the state agency, not on a list of unknown age.

Other credentials

A TWIC card is only needed to enter secure areas of maritime ports, and FHWA says operators are not required to have one in general. In many states a steerman on a steerable trailer must hold a Class A commercial driver's license. See what a steer escort is.

Business licensing is separate

Running a pilot car business involves ordinary business registration and licensing that has nothing to do with operator certification. Those rules depend on where the business is based.

Insurance has two sides

The escort vehicle needs appropriate vehicle insurance, and FHWA's checklist includes verifying insurance and registration. Separately, the carrier applying for the permit has its own obligations. In Mississippi, for example, Mississippi DOT's over-dimensional permit rules require permit applicants to provide a valid USDOT number or a certificate of insurance on file with the department of at least $500,000 single-limit liability, with exceptions at the department's discretion. That applies to the permit applicant, not to every escort driver.

How to check a state's rules

  1. List every state on the route and get the permit for each.
  2. Check each state's DOT or permit office. FHWA's overview of oversize/overweight permits is a starting point.
  3. Confirm whether your escort driver's certification is accepted there.
  4. Confirm equipment, sign and insurance requirements.
  5. If anything is unclear, call the permit office and note who you spoke to.
